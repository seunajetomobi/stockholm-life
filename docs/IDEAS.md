# Ideas that would make the game richer (proposals, none started)

Written 2026-11-21 at the owner's request ("add what I could not think of"). Every idea here is a **proposal**: nothing is built, none changes the roadmap order, and each needs the owner's yes before work. All of them gain from real Stockholm time ([REALTIME_SPEC.md](REALTIME_SPEC.md)): once the game runs on the real date and clock, the calendar can drive them for free. Any real fact (dates, fees, hours) must come from a real source and carry the date it was checked (AGENTS.md rule 8).

## Rides on the real calendar (cheap once R2 is done)
1. **Swedish red days and holiday eves.** Real public holidays (Nyårsdagen, Trettondedag jul, Långfredagen, Påskdagen, Annandag påsk, Kristi himmelsfärds dag, Nationaldagen 6 June, Midsommardagen, Alla helgons dag, Juldagen, Annandag jul) and the eves, with their real effect: shops and offices shut, SL runs the Sunday timetable, salary arrives early before a red day. Source: the Swedish Riksdag/Svenska kalenderlagen or the Swedish Tax Agency's published list, checked on a stated date.
2. **Seasonal traditions on the real date.** Valborg (30 April, bonfires), Midsommar (maypole, dancing, a Friday most people leave the city), Kräftskiva (August), Lucia (13 December, a procession), Julbord and glögg, Fettisdagen (semlor), Påsk (påskris). Each is a small scene plus a reason to be somewhere on that day.
3. **Namnsdag (name day).** The Swedish almanac has a name for every day; a small phone widget and now and then a friend wishing you a happy namnsdag. Needs a sourced almanac list.
4. **Midnight sun and polar-night light.** Daylight already comes from the sun rules; with the real date the long June evenings and the 15:00 December dusk happen on their own. Add the matching mood and energy effects (sleeplessness in June, lower mood in December, ljusterapi lamps in the shop).

## Living city
5. **Real SL disruption flavour.** The departures app already reads live SL data; add the human side: a signal failure makes you late for work and your boss reacts.
6. **Rush hours and quiet hours.** Crowds on the T-bana at 08:00 and 17:00, empty carriages at 23:00, a late-night Nattbuss.
7. **Weather that matters.** Ice on the pavement (slower, a chance of a fall), a dark and slippery Monday, rain on the bike, the first snow as an event, the lake and archipelago ice for skating (långfärdsskridsko) when the real weather allows.
8. **Fika as a mechanic.** A coffee and a bun with a colleague or a friend, with a real effect on mood and on how work relationships grow (the Swedish workplace runs on it).

## Life depth
9. **Neighbours and the building.** The tvättstuga (laundry room) booking system, a note from a neighbour about noise, the bostadsrättsförening meeting. A small, very Swedish source of daily friction and community.
10. **Allemansrätten and nature.** Weekend trips to the archipelago by Waxholmsbolaget boat, berry and mushroom picking in season, a day swimming in a lake (the real water temperature could come from a public source).
11. **Småprat and culture.** Learning Swedish unlocks richer conversations; a pronunciation mini-game; misunderstandings that are funny, not punishing.
12. **Achievements as a personal story.** A quiet "first year in Stockholm" log (first Swish, first SL card, first fika, first midsummer) instead of a score, so the game remembers your life.

## Quality of the game itself
13. **A tutorial week.** The first real days as a guided path through the essentials (personnummer, bank, BankID, SL card), already the M4 idea.
14. **Accessibility.** Larger text, colour-safe palette, reduced motion, full keyboard play, screen-reader labels, Swedish and English interface.
15. **Time-away mood.** When you return after a day away, a short summary of what happened while you were gone (a letter arrived, a friend texted, the weather turned).

## Suggested order (after R2/R3 of the real-time plan)
Red days and namnsdag (2 small, data-only), seasonal traditions (6 scenes), weather effects (3), rush hours (2), then the life-depth ideas one at a time.
